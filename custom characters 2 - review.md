# Custom Characters 2 — compatibility review

Reviewed against **The Werewolves of Miller's Hollow: The Pact** (2022 EN rulebook,
24 pp.) and against the 52-card Demon Slayer deck you have already printed.

---

## Verdict in three lines

1. **Custom 2 fits your game far better than Custom 1 did.** Custom 1 is
   One Night Ultimate Werewolf (the numbers `0, 2B, 4B, 10, 11` are ONUW wake-order
   slots — a single-night game). Custom 2 is Ultimate Werewolf / UW Deluxe, which is a
   **multi-night** game like The Pact. Most of these cards drop in with no surgery.
2. **Four cards are safe today, nine need one written ruling, four need a rewrite,
   three should be benched.** Details below.
3. **The real danger is not any single card — it is saturation.** Your deck already
   carries 4 Loners and 7 wolves. Custom 2 adds up to 4 more Loners and 4 more
   wolf-side cards. Unchecked, you get a game nobody can win.

## Card-by-card

### Green — drop in as printed

**Dream Wolf** — *doesn't wake until a werewolf dies.*
The cleanest card in the file. Adds a hidden wolf **without adding a kill**. Note for
the moderator: the Dream Wolf **is** a werewolf from minute one, so —
- Kiriya (Fox) gets a thumbs-up for them,
- Genya's (Bear Tamer) bear grunts for them,
- Himejima (Seer) sees a wolf card,
- Kokushibo (Big Bad Wolf) may **not** eat them, and a Dream Wolf death **does**
  switch off Kokushibo's second kill.
When they "replace" a dead wolf they only start waking — they do **not** inherit that
wolf's special power.

**Spellcaster** — *silence a player for the next day.*
No conflicts with any Pact rule. A silenced player still votes and still points.
Ruling to write down: silence does **not** stop the Ghost from writing letters, and
does **not** stop the Sheriff's vote counting double. Call them late in the night so
the village can't time-triangulate who woke.
(Note: this is the same effect as Custom 1's Curator "Mask of Muting" — don't run both.)

**Fruit Brute** *(suggest renaming to "Famine")* — *as the last wolf alive, you lose your
appetite and cannot feed; you must win by talking.*
Quietly the best-designed card in the whole file, and the one I'd add first. It is a
**self-nerfing wolf**: the pack's kill engine switches off the moment he is alone, so a
village that is losing badly still has a road back. It is also the natural counterweight
to Douma and the Outcast, who both *want* to be the last wolf — the Famine makes that
ambition suicidal. Rulings:
- "Cannot feed" means **no wolf kill at all** that night, not a reduced one. A Cub's
  double kill is cancelled too.
- He is only "the last wolf" when **every other wolf is dead** — a living Sleeper
  (Dream Wolf) still counts, even though it hasn't woken. So the Sleeper keeps him fed.
- **Important:** "cannot feed" is *not* "cannot decide." The Bogeyman does **not** get to
  step in on a Famine night — see below.
- The Wolf-Hound counts as a wolf for this only if they chose the wolf side.

**Nostradamus** — *predict the winning team night 1; solo win if right and alive.*
Harmless and additive. One ruling needed, because you have 4 Loners: publish the
legal list of predictions up front — *Village / Werewolves / Piper / Angel /
Manipulator / White Wolf* — and make Nostradamus pick from exactly that list.

**Hoodlum** — *name two players night 1; win if both die and you live.*
No ongoing night action, so it costs you nothing in tempo. It is a 5th Loner, though —
see the saturation warning.

### Amber — playable, but write the ruling before you deal

**Ghost** — *dies night 1, then writes one-letter clues each day.*
Lovely synergy with Sakonji (Gypsy) and the New Moon Spiritualism cards, which
already give your dead players a voice. Rulings:
- Kill the Ghost **during setup, before Cupid picks.** Otherwise Mitsuri can pair the
  Ghost and kill a Lover to a rules artifact.
- Their death is **not** a wolf kill: no Sanami (Hunter) shot, no Inoske (Knight)
  disease, Kagaya (Elder) doesn't spend a life, Murata (Devoted Servant) can't claim
  the card.
- Announce it at first dawn as its own event, separate from the night's victim.

**Lycan** — *villager who reads as a werewolf.*
Your cards are already printed, so the moderator carries this one by memory. Rulings:
- When Himejima (Seer) — or Custom 1's Revealer / Mystic Wolf / the Confessor —
  views the Lycan, **show them a Werewolf card instead.**
- Kiriya (Fox) gets a **thumbs-up** for a group containing the Lycan.
- Genya's bear **grunts** for a Lycan neighbour. (Be consistent: if it reads as a wolf,
  it reads as a wolf to everything.)
- But the Lycan is **not** a real wolf: Kokushibo *may* eat them, and Inoske's rusty
  sword does **not** infect them.

**Wolf Man** — *"the exact opposite of a Lycan"* → a werewolf who reads as a villager.
The wording is ambiguous (Bezier's own Wolf Man is village-team); I've read it the
natural way. **Play at most one of Lycan / Wolf Man per game** unless you're 20+
players. Both at once makes Himejima net-negative for the village, and with Akaza
(Wolf-Hound) and Nezuko (Wild Child) already muddying the water you have plenty of
"is that a wolf?" in the deck.

**Bogeyman** — *if the wolves can't agree, you kill for them. You win when every
night-active player is dead.*
This card does something none of your others do: **it deletes The Pact's deadlock safety
valve.** Page 8 is explicit — *"If they can't agree as a group, too bad: nobody gets eaten
tonight."* That no-kill night is one of the village's few free rides. The Bogeyman removes
it permanently, and the knife changes hands to someone with a completely different agenda.
Worth knowing before you deal it in.

The win condition is the part that needs a ruling, because **as written it is nearly
unreachable in your deck** — you own roughly eighteen night-active roles. Fix it at setup:
- At setup, write down **which roles dealt into *this* game get called at night.** That
  list, and only that list, is what the Bogeyman must clear.
- Tell the Bogeyman **the count, not the identities.** They need to know whether the job
  is four deep or eleven deep; they should not know who.
- **Does the Bogeyman count themselves?** No — they are night-active but they are the one
  left standing. Say this out loud at setup or it will be argued at 2am.
- First-night-only roles (Cupid, Thief, Sisters, Brothers, Wild Child, Judge) — I'd
  **exclude** them. They have no ongoing night presence, and including them makes the
  card impossible. Your call, but decide before the deal.
- **Call the Bogeyman every single night**, whether the wolves deadlocked or not.
  Otherwise the nights they *are* called telegraph exactly what happened in the wolf
  phase.
- A Bogeyman kill is **not** a wolf kill: it doesn't feed the Diseased, doesn't trigger
  the Knight's rusty sword, and the Elder does **not** spend a life on it.
- A Famine night is not a deadlock — the wolves decided, they just can't act. **No
  Bogeyman kill.**

**Diseased** *(renamed from "Cursed")* — *villager who turns wolf when attacked.*
The rename clears the collision with Custom 1's Cursed, so both can now sit in the deck
together. Remaining note: it overlaps with **Muzan** (Cursed Wolf-Father), whose entire
once-per-game power is this exact conversion — that's fine, they just do it by different
routes (Muzan chooses, the Diseased is automatic).

The Pact resolves the rest for you, and elegantly. Pages 10 and 15 both say infection is
stopped by *nothing*: "The Witch's healing potion does not remove the Cursed Wolf-Father's
infection" and "the Defender does not protect against... the infection." So:
- Tamayo (Witch) heals the Diseased → **the body is saved, the disease still takes.**
- Tanjiro (Defender) protects the Diseased → **death prevented, conversion still happens.**
- If Muzan bites the Diseased, the conversion happens anyway and **Muzan keeps his
  unspent bite** (tell him nothing).
- Keep both Cursed cards straight at the table: **Diseased** = turns wolf when bitten
  (Custom 2). **Cursed** = if devoured, the wolves get no kill the following night
  (Custom 1). They are opposites, which is a nice pair to run together.

**Tough Guy** — *survive an extra day if attacked at night.*
Near-duplicate of Kagaya (Elder). Distinguish them like this and both can coexist:
- **Kagaya survives silently** — the rulebook is explicit that the moderator does not
  announce it (p. 10).
- **Tough Guy's wound is announced** at dawn: "X was attacked and is dying." They play
  the whole day, can be lynched/poisoned/shot normally, and die at the following dawn.
A Witch heal on the Tough Guy cancels the wound outright.

**Doppelgänger** — *copy a player's role when they die.*
You now have **four** role-stealers: Kaigaku (Thief), Murata (Devoted Servant),
Daki (Actor), Nezuko (Wild Child). The Pact already wrote your rulings for you — reuse
the Devoted Servant text on p. 13 verbatim:
- The copied role is **brand new, powers refreshed.**
- Effects on the dead player do **not** carry over (in love, charmed, Sheriff, Town
  Crier), but the Doppelgänger's *own* infection does.
- Copying a wolf makes them a wolf, waking with the pack next night.
- Copying a **Loner works and they inherit the solo win condition** — p. 13 explicitly
  handles the Servant-becomes-Piper case (the moderator tells the new Piper who was
  already charmed). Same here.
Fun trap: a Doppelgänger who targets the Ghost becomes the Ghost on night 1.

**Wolf Cub** — *wolves get two kills the night after it dies.*
Mechanically beautiful with Kokushibo and that is exactly the problem. Kokushibo's
second kill runs *"as long as no Werewolf, Wild Child or Wolf-Hound has been
eliminated"* (p. 8) — it switches **off** at precisely the moment the Wolf Cub switches
**on**. Together the pack eats two players essentially every night of the game, start
to finish. **Run one or the other, not both.**

**Leprechaun** — *may redirect the wolf attack to a neighbour of the target.*
Very strong and worth playing, but price it correctly: to redirect, they must be woken
after the wolves and **shown the victim**, which means they learn the wolf target every
single night. That is close to Seer-tier intel. Rulings:
- One redirect per night, even against a doubled kill (Kokushibo / Wolf Cub).
- Redirect onto Tanjiro's protected player → the kill fails entirely.
- Redirect onto a wolf → **that wolf dies.**
- Redirect onto Kagaya → he spends a life.
Count this as a major village power when you're balancing the roster.

**Sasquatch** — *villager turns wolf if a day ends with no day-kill.*
In most rulesets this rarely fires. **In The Pact it is close to guaranteed**, because
you have three separate no-kill days built in:
- a tie vote eliminates nobody (p. 6),
- Aoi (Village Idiot) revealing → *"the game proceeds to night without an elimination"* (p. 11),
- the Lord's pardon, and the Heads-or-Tails event card (tails = released).
Note Hantengu (Scapegoat) cancels the tie case — with him in play a tie kills *him*, so
no trigger. If you want Sasquatch to stay a threat rather than a certainty, use
"converts on the **second** no-kill day."

**Mayor** — *vote counts double if you reveal.*
Straight duplicate of Yoriichi (Sheriff), who you've printed as a card. Two double-votes
plus the Sheriff's tie-break is too much. **Pick one.** If you want the Mayor, the clean
use is as a *replacement* for the Sheriff election in games where you don't want to
spend ten minutes electing one — the Mayor cannot then be elected Sheriff, and their
reveal is permanent and public. Also rule whether Hantengu's vote-stripping or the
Schoolmarm can shut the Mayor's double vote off (I'd say yes).

### Red — rewrite before playing

**Bloody Mary** — *"if you die, kill someone from the team that killed you each night."*
As written this is the single most game-warping card here. If she is **lynched**, the
team that killed her is *the entire village* — so a dead, untouchable player then
murders a villager every night for the rest of the game, and nothing in the game can
stop her. Two fixes, take either:
- **(a)** She only triggers if killed by **werewolves** — then she kills one wolf per
  night, a dead man's wolf-hunt. Clean and thematic.
- **(b)** If lynched, she may only kill someone who **voted for her.** Self-limiting and
  the better story.
Either way, cap her at **two kills total**, then she rests.

**Chupacabra** — *each night name a player; they die if they're a werewolf. Once all
wolves are dead, kill a player each night.* **Wins by being the last player alive.**
With the win condition settled, this card is now coherent — and it reads completely
differently. The Chupacabra is **not** a village vigilante; they are a **Loner** who
happens to be hunting the same prey as the village for the first half of the game, then
turns on them. Everything follows from that:
- The "kills a villager each night" clause is no longer a bug — it is their **win path**.
- The village cannot win while the Chupacabra lives, and **must** eventually lynch them.
- They count against your Loner cap (see saturation, below).
- A Chupacabra guess that hits a real wolf kills it **outright** — no Defender save, no
  Elder second life, no Witch heal. Rule it as a clean kill, or it becomes unusable.
- They may target the same player on consecutive nights.
- Overlap to watch: Tamayo's poison and the Barber's razor are two more "kill outside
  the vote" powers. Three in one village is a lot — drop one.
Moderator tip: the Chupacabra's early kills look exactly like a successful village, so
nobody suspects them until the wolves are gone and the deaths keep coming. That reveal
is the best moment this card has — protect it by calling them every night from night 1,
whether or not they act.

**Old Man** — *dies on night (number of werewolves + 1).*
Two failures. First, with 7 wolves in your deck that's night 8 and the game is long
over. Second and worse, it is a **precise wolf-count leak** — the village simply counts
back from the death night and learns exactly how many wolves they're hunting, which
neither The Pact nor UW ever tells them. Rewrite as a **fixed night 3**, or bench it. It
also steps on Kagaya's (Elder) thematic toes. If you do play it: his death is not a wolf
kill (no Hunter, no Knight), but a Lover **does** die of grief alongside him.

### Bench with this deck

**Insomniac** — *learn that a neighbour woke during the night.*
This works in Ultimate Werewolf, where few roles wake. Your deck has roughly fifteen
night-active roles; **nearly every neighbour wakes nearly every night**, so the
Insomniac learns nothing and will confidently mislead the village. If you want to save
it, repurpose it to something that actually carries signal: *"each night you learn
whether either living neighbour was **targeted** by a night power"* — that detects both
Tanjiro's protection and wolf attention, and it's genuinely useful.

**Lone Wolf** — *a wolf who only wins as the last wolf alive.*
Strictly a worse-designed Douma (White Wolf), who at least gets a kill power to *get*
there. The Lone Wolf has no tool at all — they just have to hope the village does their
work. **Redundant while Douma is in the deck.** If you run both, the pack is two
traitors deep and the village almost can't lose.

**Mayor** — see above. Redundant with Yoriichi.

## The four structural problems

**1. Loner saturation.** You have 4 (Douma, Chachamaru, Giyu, Kaburamaru). Custom 2
offers 4 more (Hoodlum, Nostradamus, Chupacabra, Lone Wolf) — and Custom 1 adds the
Assassin and the Tanner on top. **Ten solo win conditions exist across your full pool.**
Deal too many into one game and it ends with **no faction able to claim victory** — the
rulebook already flags exactly this failure mode for the Hunter-Lover case on p. 10.
**Cap Loners at 3 per game**, and note the Chupacabra now costs you one of those three.

**2. Wolf-kill inflation.** Kokushibo (2 kills while the pack is whole) + Wolf Cub
(2 kills once it isn't) + Muzan's infection + the Diseased's auto-conversion + Sasquatch
= the wolf team both eats double and *grows*. Pick **one** doubler and **one** converter.

**3. Dead players with live powers.** Ghost (writes), Bloody Mary (kills), Sanami
(shoots), Inoske (infects), Hantengu (strips votes), plus the Gypsy's medium and the
New Moon Spiritualism cards. Dead players in this game are already loud. Ghost is worth
it; Bloody Mary needs the cap above.

**4. Night length.** The Pact's own call table is ~18 stops. Custom 2 adds up to 8 more.
At 20+ stops a single night runs past ten minutes and the table goes cold. **Hard-cap
your night at roughly 20 calls**, and don't layer Custom 1 on top of Custom 2 in the
same game.

## Three rosters that actually work

**Safe (12–18 players).** Add: Ghost, Spellcaster, Tough Guy, Dream Wolf, Nostradamus.
Nothing here breaks a rule, night grows by only three stops, and the Ghost + Gypsy
combination gives your dead players something to do. Start here.

**Spicy (18–26).** The above, plus: Lycan **or** Wolf Man (not both), Doppelgänger,
Leprechaun, Hoodlum, and Wolf Cub **in place of** Kokushibo.

**Chaos (26+).** The above, plus: Sasquatch, Diseased, **Bogeyman**, Bloody Mary
capped at two kills, and the Chupacabra as a Loner. Expect a long night phase and at least one argument.

**Bench in all three:** Insomniac, Old Man, Mayor. *(Lone Wolf / Outcast comes off the
bench the moment you add Famine — the two of them together are a genuinely good
wolf-side subplot.)*
